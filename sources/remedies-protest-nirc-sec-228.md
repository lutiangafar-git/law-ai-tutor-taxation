# Source text
Such assessment may be protested administratively by filing a request for reconsideration or reinvestigation within thirty (30) days from receipt of the assessment in such form and manner as may be prescribed by implementing rules and regulations. Within sixty (60) days from filing of the protest, all relevant supporting documents shall have been submitted; otherwise, the assessment shall become final. If the protest is denied in whole or in part, or is not acted upon within one hundred eighty (180) days from submission of documents, the taxpayer adversely affected by the decision or inaction may appeal to the Court of Tax Appeals within thirty (30) days from receipt of the said decision, or from the lapse of one hundred eighty (180)-day period; otherwise, the decision shall become final, executory and demandable.

# Key points for the tutor (reviewer-approved)
- The taxpayer protests by filing a request for reconsideration or reinvestigation within 30 days from receipt of the assessment (Sec. 228).
- All relevant supporting documents must be submitted within 60 days from filing the protest; otherwise the assessment becomes final (Sec. 228).
- If the protest is denied, or not acted upon within 180 days from submission of documents, the taxpayer may appeal to the CTA within 30 days from receipt of the decision or from the lapse of the 180-day period (Sec. 228).
- Missing the 30-day protest period makes the assessment final; missing the 30-day appeal period makes the decision final, executory and demandable.

# Recitation drill: application fact pattern
Scenario: Mabuhay Corp. received a final assessment notice on 1 June and filed its protest on 15 July. Was the protest filed on time?
Model answer: No. Under Sec. 228, a protest must be filed within 30 days from receipt of the assessment. From 1 June, the 30 days ended on 1 July. A protest filed on 15 July is late, so the assessment became final.

# Problem drill: exam or bar-style problem
Problem: (Original bar-style problem. Related issues were asked in the 2016 and 2018 Bar Examinations, Taxation Law.) Ilaw Corp. received a final assessment notice from the BIR and filed a request for reinvestigation 25 days later. Twenty days after filing the protest, it submitted all its supporting documents. The Commissioner did not act on the protest. Ilaw Corp. asks: (a) Did it protest on time? (b) If the Commissioner still does not act, when may it go to the Court of Tax Appeals, and until when? Explain.
Model answer:
- Answer: (a) Yes, the protest was on time. (b) Ilaw Corp. may appeal to the CTA once 180 days have passed from its submission of documents without action, and must do so within 30 days after that 180-day period lapses.
- Legal basis: Under Sec. 228 of the NIRC, an assessment may be protested by a request for reconsideration or reinvestigation within 30 days from receipt; supporting documents must be submitted within 60 days from filing the protest. If the protest is not acted upon within 180 days from submission of documents, the taxpayer may appeal to the CTA within 30 days from the lapse of the 180-day period; otherwise, the decision becomes final, executory and demandable.
- Application: Ilaw Corp. filed its request for reinvestigation 25 days after receipt, within the 30 days. It submitted its documents 20 days after filing, within the 60 days. The 180-day period for the Commissioner to act therefore runs from the day it submitted the documents. If the Commissioner does not act within those 180 days, Ilaw Corp. may treat the inaction as a ground for appeal and must file with the CTA within 30 days from the lapse of the 180 days.
- Conclusion: Ilaw Corp.'s protest is timely, and on the Commissioner's inaction it must appeal to the CTA within 30 days after the 180-day period, counted from its submission of documents, expires.

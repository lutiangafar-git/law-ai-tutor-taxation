# Source text
Section 234. Exemptions from Real Property Tax. - The following are exempted from payment of the real property tax: (a) Real property owned by the Republic of the Philippines or any of its political subdivisions except when the beneficial use thereof has been granted, for consideration or otherwise, to a taxable person; (b) Charitable institutions, churches, parsonages or convents appurtenant thereto, mosques, non-profit or religious cemeteries and all lands, buildings, and improvements actually, directly, and exclusively used for religious, charitable or educational purposes;

Article VI, Section 28. (...) (3) Charitable institutions, churches and personages or convents appurtenant thereto, mosques, non-profit cemeteries, and all lands, buildings, and improvements, actually, directly, and exclusively used for religious, charitable, or educational purposes shall be exempt from taxation.

# Key points for the tutor (reviewer-approved)
- Lands, buildings and improvements actually, directly and exclusively used for religious, charitable or educational purposes are exempt from real property tax (LGC Sec. 234(b); Const. Art. VI, Sec. 28(3)).
- The test is USE, not ownership: property of a church or charity is exempt only to the extent it is actually, directly and exclusively used for those purposes.
- A portion leased out for commercial purposes is not used exclusively for religious, charitable or educational purposes, so that portion is taxable.
- Government-owned property is exempt, except when its beneficial use has been granted, for consideration or otherwise, to a taxable person (Sec. 234(a)).

# Recitation drill: application fact pattern
Scenario: A city owns a public market building and leases the whole building for 25 years to a private corporation that runs it for profit. The corporation says the building is exempt because the city owns it. Is it correct?
Model answer: No. Under Sec. 234(a), real property owned by a political subdivision is exempt except when its beneficial use has been granted, for consideration or otherwise, to a taxable person. The city granted the beneficial use to a private, taxable corporation, so the exemption does not apply.

# Problem drill: exam or bar-style problem
Problem: (Original bar-style problem. Related issues were asked in the 2010, 2016 and 2017 Bar Examinations, Taxation Law.) San Isidro Parish owns a two-storey building beside its church. The ground floor is leased to a pharmacy and a fast-food outlet. The second floor is used as the parish office and for free catechism classes. The city assessor assessed real property tax on the entire building. The parish argues that the whole building is exempt because a church owns it. Who is correct? Explain.
Model answer:
- Answer: Neither is fully correct. The second floor is exempt, but the leased ground floor is subject to real property tax.
- Legal basis: Under Sec. 234(b) of the Local Government Code and Art. VI, Sec. 28(3) of the Constitution, lands, buildings and improvements actually, directly and exclusively used for religious, charitable or educational purposes are exempt from real property tax. The exemption depends on the actual use of the property, not on who owns it.
- Application: The second floor is used as the parish office and for free catechism classes, a use that is actually, directly and exclusively religious, so it is exempt. The ground floor, though owned by the parish, is leased to a pharmacy and a fast-food outlet for commercial purposes; it is not used exclusively for religious purposes. Church ownership alone does not make the leased portion exempt.
- Conclusion: The city may collect real property tax only on the leased ground floor; the assessment on the second floor should be cancelled.

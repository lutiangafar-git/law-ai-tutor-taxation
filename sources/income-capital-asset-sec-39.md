# Source text
(1) Capital Assets. - the term 'capital assets' means property held by the taxpayer (whether or not connected with his trade or business), but does not include stock in trade of the taxpayer or other property of a kind which would properly be included in the inventory of the taxpayer if on hand at the close of the taxable year, or property held by the taxpayer primarily for sale to customers in the ordinary course of his trade or business, or property used in the trade or business, of a character which is subject to the allowance for depreciation provided in Subsection (F) of Section 34; or real property used in trade or business of the taxpayer.

# Key points for the tutor (reviewer-approved)
- Capital assets are all property held by the taxpayer, whether or not connected with trade or business, EXCEPT the excluded kinds (Sec. 39(A)(1)).
- Excluded, and therefore ordinary assets: (1) stock in trade or inventory; (2) property held primarily for sale to customers in the ordinary course of trade or business; (3) depreciable property used in trade or business; (4) real property used in trade or business.
- The classification depends on how the taxpayer holds and uses the property, not on what the property is.
- The same lot can be an ordinary asset for a real estate dealer and a capital asset for someone who merely owns it.

# Recitation drill: application fact pattern
Scenario: Tierra Homes Inc., a real estate developer, sells a house and lot from its subdivision to a buyer. Is the house and lot a capital asset of Tierra Homes?
Model answer: No. Under Sec. 39(A)(1), property held primarily for sale to customers in the ordinary course of trade or business is excluded from capital assets. Tierra Homes is in the business of selling subdivision lots, so the house and lot is its ordinary asset.

# Problem drill: exam or bar-style problem
Problem: (Original bar-style problem. Related issues were asked in the 2015 and 2019 Bar Examinations, Taxation Law.) Dr. Dana Cruz, a practising dentist, inherited a vacant lot in Batangas from her father. She never built on it, never used it in her dental practice, and has never bought or sold any other real property. She sold the lot this year. The BIR examiner treated the lot as an ordinary asset, reasoning that Dr. Cruz is engaged in business through her dental practice. Is the lot a capital asset or an ordinary asset? Explain.
Model answer:
(Answer) The lot is a capital asset of Dr. Cruz.

(Legal basis) Under Sec. 39(A)(1) of the NIRC, capital assets are property held by the taxpayer, whether or not connected with trade or business, except stock in trade or inventory, property held primarily for sale to customers in the ordinary course of trade or business, depreciable property used in trade or business, and real property used in trade or business.

(Application) Dr. Cruz is in the business of dentistry, not real estate; she has never bought or sold other real property, so the lot is not stock in trade or held for sale to customers. It was never used in her dental practice, so it is not real property used in trade or business. That she has a business of some kind does not make every property she owns an ordinary asset; what matters is how this property was held and used.

(Conclusion) Because the lot falls under none of the exclusions in Sec. 39(A)(1), it is a capital asset, and the examiner's treatment is wrong.

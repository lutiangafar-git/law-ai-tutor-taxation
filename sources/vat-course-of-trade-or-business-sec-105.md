# Source text
Section 105. Persons Liable. - Any person who, in the course of trade or business, sells barters, exchanges, leases goods or properties, renders services, and any person who imports goods shall be subject to the value-added tax (VAT) imposed in Sections 106 to 108 of this Code. The value-added tax is an indirect tax and the amount of tax may be shifted or passed on to the buyer, transferee or lessee of the goods, properties or services. This rule shall likewise apply to existing contracts of sale or lease of goods, properties or services at the time of the effectivity of Republic Act No. 7716. The phrase 'in the course of trade or business' means the regular conduct or pursuit of a commercial or an economic activity, including transactions incidental thereto, by any person regardless of whether or not the person engaged therein is a nonstock, nonprofit private organization

# Key points for the tutor (reviewer-approved)
- VAT applies to any person who, in the course of trade or business, sells, barters, exchanges or leases goods or properties, or renders services, and to any person who imports goods (Sec. 105).
- "In the course of trade or business" means the REGULAR conduct or pursuit of a commercial or economic activity, including incidental transactions (Sec. 105).
- An isolated, casual sale outside any regular commercial activity is not in the course of trade or business.
- Being a nonstock, nonprofit organization does not by itself take a regular commercial activity outside VAT (Sec. 105).
- VAT is an indirect tax: it may be shifted or passed on to the buyer, but the seller remains the person liable (Sec. 105).

# Recitation drill: application fact pattern
Scenario: A nonstock, nonprofit school foundation regularly runs a bookstore selling school supplies to the public all year round. It says it is not subject to VAT because it is nonprofit. Is it correct?
Model answer: No. Under Sec. 105, "in the course of trade or business" covers the regular conduct of a commercial activity by any person, regardless of whether it is a nonstock, nonprofit organization. Running a bookstore all year round is a regular commercial activity, so the sales are subject to VAT unless a specific exemption applies.

# Problem drill: exam or bar-style problem
Problem: (Original bar-style problem. Related issues were asked in the 2014 and 2019 Bar Examinations, Taxation Law.) Ramon, a bank employee, bought a car for his family's personal use. After four years, he sold it to his officemate for P600,000. He has never sold any other car and is not engaged in any business. The BIR assessed Ramon for VAT on the sale. Is the sale subject to VAT? Explain.
Model answer:
- Answer: No. Ramon's sale of his family car is not subject to VAT.
- Legal basis: Under Sec. 105 of the NIRC, VAT is imposed on a person who sells goods or properties "in the course of trade or business", which means the regular conduct or pursuit of a commercial or an economic activity, including transactions incidental to it.
- Application: Ramon is an employee, not engaged in any business. He bought the car for his family's personal use and sold it once, after four years. This is an isolated, casual sale, not part of any regular commercial or economic activity, nor incidental to one. It is therefore not made in the course of trade or business.
- Conclusion: Because the sale was not in the course of trade or business, it falls outside Sec. 105, and the VAT assessment is wrong.
